What does this gene do to the repetitive genome?

Roughly half the human genome is repeated, mobile DNA. Cells spend real energy keeping it switched off; when that machinery fails it switches back on, and that is tied to inflammation and ageing.

Ask that question of any gene today and five separate things are in your way. This is which of them already have answers, which do not, and what we built for the ones that do not.

The problems

Five obstacles, and they are not the same kind of problem

#ProblemWhy it blocks the question
1Repeats are thrown away by defaultStandard RNA-seq pipelines discard multi-mapping reads. Most of the repetitive genome is multi-mapping, so it is invisible unless you deliberately handle it
2Transposable elements are not entities in biomedical knowledge graphsYou cannot query a relationship whose object does not exist in the schema
3Perturbation data was never read out on repeatsENCODE holds 1,114 knockdowns across 570 genes, reads public. The measurement was paid for and never made
4Predictions rarely say where they came fromIf a tool tells you gene X silences element Y, you usually cannot tell whether that is a measurement, a curation, a sentence in an abstract, or a model's guess
5Nothing says “not enough evidence”Systems that always answer cannot tell you which answers to distrust

1 and 3 are engineering. 2 is a schema problem. 4 and 5 are epistemic — and they are the ones that do not go away with more data.

The gap

What is left once you credit all of that

Every atlas above answers where transposable elements are active — which tissue, which tumour, which cell. None of them answers what perturbation changed them, through what mechanism, and what to test next. Observational versus interventional. That axis is comparatively empty.

And the two epistemic problems, 4 and 5, are untouched by any of it. So the residual gap is narrow and specific:

GapWhat we built for it
a perturbation read out on the repeat genome becomes a signed, sourced relationship27 measured edges from re-analysed ENCODE knockdowns, each carrying accession, statistic, date and a stable id
those relationships sit in one schema with curated mechanism, so they can be reasoned over9,366 typed edges across four evidence layers; transposable elements are first-class nodes
a conclusion's trust level is computed, not assertedtier = the weakest link on the derivation path, and the path is printed
the system can declinebest_tier: none is a supported output, not an error

Stated honestly: every component here has precedent. The assembly did not surface in two independent searches — which is a `NOT_FOUND`, and still not proof of absence.

Result 1 — real data

The TE compartment moves as a whole

Look at DNMT1. Zero individual TE families clear FDR — best padj 0.41 — yet the whole compartment is displaced. A per-feature search finds nothing here; the compartment statistic finds a coherent shift. Result 2 — the proof

Not a lookup: the answer is a chain you can audit

TET2 has no transposon annotation in the Gene Ontology and no knockdown in ENCODE. The prediction is nevertheless specific, directional, and traceable to one real measurement — and every edge is labelled with the kind of evidence it is.

A conclusion's tier is the weakest link on its path, so this answer is inferred, never measured. Run it yourself: python src/te_reason.py TET2 --proofs

Result 3 — refusal

It recognises missing evidence, fixes it, and still accepts a null

The interferon question came back refused, not answered: the reference held 17% of the signature, below the coverage guard. So we built a targeted 62 Mb reference, took coverage to 77%, and re-ran — and the answer was still a null.

The null then got a mechanism: in K562 the cytosolic sensors are barely expressed. The wiring and the sprinklers are installed; the smoke detectors are not. So the claim is not “TE derepression does not cause interferon” — it is this dataset cannot decisively test it, and here is the cell type that could.

Three more things were built, measured and not shipped: a literature extractor at precision 0.109, link prediction with no constructible held-out set, and the p-values retracted on the previous slide. best_tier: none is a supported output, not an error path.

Result 4 — what it is for

It tells you which experiment to run next

Remove TRIM28 — the largest measured effect in the dataset — and the reasoning is unchanged, because a scaffold has no writer annotation and cannot ground a mark. Remove SETDB1 and 91% of the inference disappears. Four edges support the entire derived layer.

That converts “we need more data” into a named experiment: more writers and erasers of these two marks. We checked — of 40 such enzymes in the graph, ENCODE has knockdowns for three, and we have used two. The public well is nearly dry, and that is the result.

Honestly

What is weak, what compute would buy, and the ask

The binding limitations

• the whole 5mC arm rests on one contrast, 1.5 M reads across chr1+chr19 — and chr19 is the most gene-dense, Alu-rich chromosome
• SETDB1's efficiency not confirmed, and it anchors 3 of 4 grounding edges
• only 9 of 55 silencing-machinery genes measurable at this reference scope
• subfamily resolution, not loci — the field is moving to loci
• two replicates per arm caps any sample-level p at 0.333
• no time, position, dose or feedback — those context columns read not_applicable throughout

What compute buys, costed

A genome-wide re-run at 30 M reads fixes the first four at once: retires the two-chromosome bias, lifts machinery coverage, settles the SETDB1 question, and retires the interferon coverage guard because its 725 genes become present by construction.

The pipeline is written, containerised and dry-run validated (src/modal_app.py). Pre-flight found three defects that would each have wasted the run — including a setup step that reported success while never building the annotation the counter needs.

Not launched today, deliberately. The shared reference build is a 1–2.5 h critical path and there are 90 minutes. Spending credits on a run that cannot finish would be theatre.

Two adversarial prior-art passes, 279 abstracts, 198 full texts, five novelty claims retracted — including one we liked. And one search returned a clean zero we believed: the paper existed, and its fourth author was in the room.

The ask is specific: writers and erasers of H3K9me3 and 5mC from outside ENCODE. Everything else is computed, in the repository, and reproducible from one command.

Appendix

What a dynamical layer could add to knowledge-graph research

Not specific to this project. Four things no knowledge graph can represent, in any domain — consequences of the representation, not gaps in the data.

What a graph cannot holdWhyWhat it costs you
timeforward chaining reaches a fixpoint, and a fixpoint has no clock“X silences Y” cannot distinguish an immediate effect from one taking ten cell divisions
positiona node is an identity, not a coordinateone node here stands for up to 104,272 scattered loci with no spatial relation to each other
dosea signed edge has no magnitude axispartial and complete loss of function are the same edge
feedbackchaining to a fixpoint does not model a cycle — it ignores oneour cGAS path is a cycle, so its acyclic reading is known to be wrong

A cellular automaton is local rules applied over time on a lattice. It is natively temporal, spatial, quantitative and cyclic — exactly the four axes a graph gives up in exchange for provenance.

The track record is not speculative: Turing 1952 (local rules generate pattern), Moe 1964 (a cardiac automaton reproducing fibrillation), Kauffman 1969 (random Boolean networks settling into few stable states), Li 2004 (yeast cell cycle), Anderson Cell 2006 (tumour morphology under microenvironmental selection), PhysiCell 2018.

Appendix

How the two would compose — and the conditions for believing it

The asymmetry, stated precisely

The automaton supplies structure with no referents: its cells are not loci, its states are not molecules, nothing in it has a source, and nothing in it could be falsified by an experiment.

The graph supplies referents with evidence and no dynamics: every node is a named entity, every edge carries a citation and a tier, and none of it moves.

Complements, not competitors — the useful direction is the automaton proposing phenomena to look for and the graph naming which entities could instantiate them.

One case where it would earn its place

A deterministic rule engine gives one direction per gene for all targets. It cannot produce a split — and our own cohort splits: DNMT3A-mutant carriers divide into RTE-high and RTE-low by which mutation they carry.

Bimodality from identical parameters is a generic property of bistable stochastic local-rule models. So it is a candidate explanation for a pattern the current model cannot generate at all.

Three conditions, or it is decoration. (1) Pre-commit the biological prediction before looking at the graph — in 9,366 relationships you will always find something analogous to any principle you bring. (2) The abstract side needs its own null model: “recovery uses few dimensions” may just be what search in low dimensions always looks like. (3) Simulated output carries a tier below inferred and can never ground a rule — using it to fill edges would be the link prediction we already refused, and a simulation does not create validation data.

And a falsifier fixed in advance: the first buildable version predicts per-locus derepression depends on distance to the nearest nucleation site and local density, not on subfamily identity — refutable with a locus-level quantifier. If it fails, the model is dropped, not tuned. Design in docs/CELLULAR_AUTOMATA.md; nothing from it is implemented, and nothing from it is in any number on the previous slides.
