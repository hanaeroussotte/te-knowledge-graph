From simple rules to a real biological question

A cellular automaton is the simplest thing that can be called a model of a living system: a row of cells, each one recolouring itself from its own colour and its two neighbours', over and over.

It produces rules you could not have guessed — and it cannot tell you anything about a named gene. This is about joining that to a graph of real, cited, measured biology, and what happened when we did.

Hanae Roussotte · with a cellular-automata collaborator · biology knowledge-graph track

1 — The method

Eight numbers decide everything

Each panel starts from one black cell and applies its own eight-case rule. Nothing differs between them but those eight outputs — and you get uniform growth, an exact fractal, chaos, and localised structures that collide and compute.

Wolfram's classification of these four behaviours (Rev. Mod. Phys. 1983) is the founding result: you cannot read the behaviour off the rule. You have to run it.

2 — Why biology cares

Local rules reproduce phenomena no equation was written for

The track record is real

Turing, 1952 — local interaction alone generates stripes and spots, before anything was known about the molecules.
Moe, 1964 — a cellular automaton of cardiac tissue reproducing atrial fibrillation.
Kauffman, 1969 — random Boolean networks settling into a small number of stable states, proposed as cell types.
Li et al., 2004 — the yeast cell-cycle network is robust as a network.
Anderson et al., Cell 2006 — a hybrid automaton showing tumour morphology driven by microenvironmental selection.
PhysiCell, 2018 — the modern multicellular simulator, in routine use.

What the method actually buys

You can enumerate. Every possible perturbation, every possible intervention — impossible in any real organism.

You get generic phenomena. Bistability, travelling waves, spontaneous recovery, threshold effects — these emerge from the structure of local interaction, not from the biochemistry, so they should appear in any system with that structure.

You find rules, not parameters. The output is a statement of the form “systems like this recover in this way”.

That is a genuinely different kind of knowledge from a measurement, and the field has been extracting it for seventy years.

3 — The frontier

The metamodel of medicine, run end to end

Following Wolfram's Foundations of Medicine framing: the organism is a pattern that grows and dies (ours at step 117), a disease is changing one cell, a treatment is changing a second. Now enumerate.

All 3,447 possible diseases

8% heal themselves with no treatment · 10% shorten life · 50% extend it · 32% grow without bound.

Wolfram's own organism self-heals at 8.7%; ours, found independently, at 8.2%. A property of the class, not of one lucky rule.

All 1,479 possible treatments

6 cure (0.4%) · 195 (13%) cause unbounded, tumour-like growth · 79 are worse than the disease.

And every cure leaves 1,622 cells changed. The lifetime is restored exactly; the life is not the same life.

4 — The limitation

Now name a gene

Everything on the previous slide is true of a pattern of coloured squares. The four colours are not molecules. Cell 60 is not a locus. There is no gene, no protein, no citation, and nothing that could be looked up or falsified by an experiment.

What it cannot do

• Say which real perturbation corresponds to its disease
• Say which real molecule could be its treatment
• Be checked against a measurement
• Carry provenance — nothing in it has a source
• Be wrong in a way anyone could detect

And the honest version of its strength

A model with many free parameters, fitted to nothing, will reproduce some qualitative phenomenon whatever the biology. That is why “our simulation looks like the disease” is the weakest sentence in computational biology.

The way out is not a better simulation. It is to make the model's statements point at named entities that carry evidence.

The method has rules without nouns. So: find something that is nothing but nouns, each with a citation.

5 — The complement

A knowledge graph is nouns with evidence attached

curated9,263 edgesGene Ontology · STRING v12 · Reactome · Complex Portal
mechanism44 edgeswhich enzyme writes or erases which chromatin mark
measured27 edgesour own re-analysis — signed, dated, and reproducible
literaturetiered lowestextracted, unvalidated, and never permitted to ground a rule

Every edge carries a source, a retrieval date, a stable id and an evidence tier. Rules chain forward to a fixpoint; a conclusion's tier is the weakest link on its path, and every answer prints the derivation that produced it.

So the two halves are complementary in a precise way, not a rhetorical one: the automaton supplies structure with no referents; the graph supplies referents with evidence and no dynamics. The graph can name what a perturbation is, what a candidate treatment would be, and refuse when it cannot.

Structural audit, make validate: 42 metrics. 0 schema violations across 9,366 typed edges, 0 duplicate triples, 100% provenance, 97.6% in one connected component. It also found a real defect — one gene–TE pair asserted in both directions, both edges from the same abstract. Kept, not deleted: that is the literature layer's error rate becoming visible.

6 — The example

Transposable elements: the case where the nouns were missing

The biology

Roughly half the human genome is repeated, mobile DNA. Cells spend real energy keeping it switched off, using chemical marks. When that machinery fails the elements switch back on — and that is tied to inflammation and ageing.

Excluded twice over

Out of the graphs. The major biomedical knowledge graphs do not model transposable elements as first-class entities — PrimeKG has 10 principal node types, Hetionet 11, and none is a transposable element.

Out of the experiments. ENCODE holds 1,114 knockdowns across 570 genes, public reads. Standard pipelines discard multi-mapping reads — which is most of the repetitive genome. The measurement was paid for and never made.

This is why it is the right test case. The automaton has no nouns; for transposable elements, biology had no nouns either. We had to build them before either method could say anything.

8 — Attacked

The one result that had to survive, and what it cost

Our primary readout is normalisation-invariant by construction: Δ = median log₂FC over TE subfamilies − median over protein-coding genes, within one contrast. Size factors are per-sample and multiplicative, so they shift both sets equally on the log scale and cancel in the difference.

An external reviewer found the real flaw: our p-value treated ~350 correlated TE families as independent evidence. We measured the inflation rather than arguing about it. Mean pairwise correlation 0.006 to 0.48 → variance inflation 4× to 155× — in two of three contrasts, 322 families carry about two families' worth of independent information. Those p-values are retracted.

9 — Reasoning

One measurement reaches genes nobody assayed

The rules

R1 grounds a mark on a TE from a measured writer effect · R2 transfers to other writers of that mark · R3 inverts the sign for erasers · R4 needs complex consensus · R6 demands TE derepression and inflammation in the same contrast — and has never fired.

89 derived edges from 27 measured ones, each printing its proof.

The worked case

From the DNMT1 measurement plus two Gene Ontology records, the engine derives DNMT3A loss raises TEs and TET2 loss lowers them — opposite signs on one axis, having seen data for neither gene.

That hypothesis is published and I am a co-author (GeroScience 2026). This instruments my own prior finding; it does not confirm it.

What is not trivial: the rule commits to a direction the paper's own hypothesis does not state — TEs go down when TET2 is lost — and the measured cohort trends that way, across a different quantifier, resolution, perturbation type and tissue.

10 — Robustness

How much of the reasoning rests on how little

Remove SETDB1 and the derived layer collapses from 89 edges to 8. Remove TRIM28 — the largest measured effect in the dataset — and nothing changes, because a scaffold has no writer annotation and cannot satisfy R1. Only four edges ground the entire inference layer.

This is where the graph stops being a database and becomes an experimental-design tool: it converts “we need more data” into more writers and erasers of these two marks. We then checked — of 40 such enzymes in the graph, ENCODE has knockdowns for three, and we have used two. The public well is nearly dry, which is itself the result.

The interferon arm is the instructive one. We built a panel reference and took type-I interferon coverage from 0.17 to 0.77 — and the answer was still a null, now with a mechanism: in K562 the cytosolic sensors are barely expressed. CGAS 0.21, ZBP1 0.26, TLR3 0.00 CPM, while the transducers run at 270–800.

The wiring and the sprinklers are installed; the smoke detectors are not. So the claim is not “TE derepression does not cause interferon” — it is this dataset cannot decisively test it, and here is the cell type that could.

12 — The join

Why the two halves need each other, stated precisely

What the automaton gives the graph

A question the graph cannot pose: what would a treatment even look like? Rare, side-effect-laden, 13% actively harmful, and not reachable by interpolation. It also names phenomena to go looking for — bistability being the sharpest, because our data already contains an unexplained split.

What the graph gives the automaton

Nouns with evidence. A candidate rescue is not “dimension 3” but a named enzyme with a Gene Ontology annotation, a measured effect, and a tier. And a refusal when there is nothing to say.

Both halves inherit the same limit: computational irreducibility — the only way to know what a rule does is to run it. But irreducible processes contain pockets of reducibility, and a provenance-bearing graph is precisely a map of them. The refusals mark where the map ends.

Which also explains the bistability match. In the metamodel, rule cases never used while the organism is healthy make variants indistinguishable until a perturbation arrives. In our own cohort, DNMT3A carriers split RTE-high versus RTE-low by which mutation they carry — same gene, same diagnosis, opposite behaviour. Generic in the model; real in the patients.
