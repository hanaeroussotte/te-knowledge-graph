What does this gene do to the repetitive genome?

Half the human genome is repeated, mobile DNA. Cells spend real energy keeping it switched off; when that machinery fails it switches back on, and that is tied to inflammation and ageing.

Ask that question of any gene and there is nothing to ask it against. Transposable elements are missing from the knowledge graphs, and missing from the experiments that could have answered it.

Absent from the graphs

The major biomedical knowledge graphs do not model transposable elements as first-class entities. PrimeKG has 10 principal node types, Hetionet 11. None is a transposable element.

Absent from the experiments

ENCODE holds 1,114 knockdowns across 570 genes, reads public. Standard pipelines discard multi-mapping reads — which is most of the repetitive genome. The measurement was paid for and never made.

Attacked

The one result that had to survive, and what it cost

The primary readout is normalisation-invariant by construction: Δ = median log₂FC over TE subfamilies − median over protein-coding genes, within one contrast. Size factors are per-sample and multiplicative, so they shift both sets equally on the log scale and cancel in the difference.

An external reviewer found the real flaw: our p-value treated ~350 correlated TE families as independent evidence. We measured the inflation instead of arguing. Mean pairwise correlation 0.006 to 0.48 → variance inflation 4× to 155× — in two of three contrasts, 322 families carry about two families' worth of independent information. Those p-values are retracted.

TRIM28's whole-compartment claim did not survive — a permuted labelling beat the real one — and is now an effect size only. Its per-feature AluY result is a different statistic and stands. DNMT1 is the anchor the TET2 answer on slide 2 rests on, which is why this is the slide that mattered.

Robustness

How much of the reasoning rests on how little

Remove SETDB1 and the derived layer collapses from 89 edges to 8. Remove TRIM28 — the largest measured effect in the dataset — and nothing changes, because a scaffold has no writer annotation and cannot satisfy R1. Only four edges ground the entire inference layer.

This is where the graph stops being a database and becomes an experimental-design tool: it turns “we need more data” into more writers and erasers of these two marks. We checked — of 40 such enzymes in the graph, ENCODE has knockdowns for three, and we have used two. The public well is nearly dry, which is itself the result.

The interferon arm is the instructive one. We built a panel reference and took type-I interferon coverage from 0.17 to 0.77 — and the answer was still a null, now with a mechanism: in K562 the cytosolic sensors are barely expressed. CGAS 0.21, ZBP1 0.26, TLR3 0.00 CPM, while the transducers run at 270–800.

The wiring and the sprinklers are installed; the smoke detectors are not. So the claim is not “TE derepression does not cause interferon” — it is this dataset cannot decisively test it, and here is the cell type that could.

best_tier: none is a supported output, not an error path. That is the whole design.
