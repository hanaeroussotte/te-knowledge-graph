Evidence-Traceable Neurosymbolic Reasoning
for Transposable-Element Regulation

Half of your genome is repeated DNA that cells work constantly to keep switched off. When that machinery fails, those elements switch back on — and that has been tied to inflammation and ageing.

This system re-analyses public perturbation experiments for what they did to the repetitive genome, turns each result into a relationship that carries its own evidence, reasons over the result, and tells you when the evidence is not enough.

The gap

Transposable elements are excluded twice over

Out of the knowledge graphs

The major general-purpose biomedical graphs do not model transposable elements as first-class entities. PrimeKG has 10 principal node types; Hetionet has 11. None is a transposable element.

Nearest precedents: TEKRABber builds TE–KRAB-ZFP networks, PlaNC-TE is a plant TE knowledgebase. Neither is perturbational.

Out of the experiments

ENCODE holds 1,114 knockdown experiments across 570 genes. The reads are public. Almost nobody re-counts them against the repeat annotation, because the standard pipeline discards multi-mapping reads — which is most of the repetitive genome.

The measurement exists in data already paid for. It was never made.

So the question “what does this gene do to transposable elements?” has no substrate to be asked against. That is what we built.

We ran that efficiency check on ourselves today, and it cost us: SETDB1 — the sole anchor for H3K9me3 — cannot be confirmed to have worked. Verdict is not confirmed, not failed: CRISPRi acts on transcription, we read a shallow snapshot, and the knockdown did produce the ERV results.

The headline, attacked

One result had to survive, and it did — weaker than it started

Our primary readout is normalisation-invariant by construction: Δ = median log₂FC over TE subfamilies − median over protein-coding genes, within one contrast. Size factors are per-sample and multiplicative, so they shift both sets equally on the log scale and cancel in the difference.

An external reviewer found the real flaw: our p-value treated ~350 correlated TE families as independent evidence. We measured the inflation instead of arguing. Mean pairwise correlation 0.006–0.48 → variance inflation 4× to 155×. In the two CRISPRi contrasts, 322 families carry about two families' worth of independent information. Those p-values are retracted.

The reasoning layer

One measurement propagates to genes nobody assayed

What fires

R1 grounds a mark on a TE from a measured writer effect · R2 transfers to other writers of the same mark · R3 inverts the sign for erasers · R4 requires complex consensus · R6 demands TE derepression and inflammation in the same contrast — and has never fired.

89 derived edges from 27 measured ones. Each prints its proof tree.

The worked example

From the DNMT1 measurement plus two Gene Ontology records, the engine derives DNMT3A loss raises TEs and TET2 loss lowers them — opposite signs on one axis, having never seen data for either gene.

The hypothesis is published, and I am a co-author (GeroScience 2026). So this instruments my own prior finding; it does not confirm it.

What is not trivial: the rule commits to a direction the paper's own hypothesis does not state — TEs go down in TET2 — and the measured data trends that way, across a different quantifier, resolution, perturbation type and tissue.

Robustness

We asked how much of our own reasoning rests on how little

Remove SETDB1 and the derived layer collapses from 89 edges to 8. Remove TRIM28 — the largest measured effect in the whole dataset — and nothing changes at all, because a scaffold has no writer annotation and cannot satisfy R1.

Only four edges ground the entire inference layer. No hold-out ever flipped a direction; genes either keep their prediction or return best_tier: none.

That converts “we need more data” into a specific experimental request: more writers and erasers of the same two marks — not more scaffolds. And we checked: of 40 mark enzymes in the graph, ENCODE has knockdowns for three, and we have used two. The public well is nearly dry, which is itself the finding.

The interferon arm is the instructive one. We built a panel reference, took type-I interferon coverage from 0.17 to 0.77 — and the answer was still a null, now with a mechanism: in K562 the cytosolic sensors are barely expressed. CGAS 0.21, ZBP1 0.26, TLR3 0.00 CPM, while the transducers run at 270–800.

The wiring and the sprinklers are installed; the smoke detectors are not. So the honest claim is not “TE derepression does not cause interferon” — it is this dataset cannot decisively test it, and here is the cell type that could.

The inflammatory-response set still fails the 0.6 coverage guard at 0.535. The guard is still refusing, after the fix.

Collaboration

An abstract theory of treatment, joined to a real measurement

What enumeration shows

Organism = a pattern from a simple rule, dying at step 117. Disease = change one cell → dies at 70. Treatment = change a second. Of 3,447 possible diseases, 8% heal themselves. Of 1,479 possible treatments, 6 cure and 195 (13%) cause unbounded, tumour-like growth — and every cure leaves 1,622 cells changed.

Why it belongs here

Both sides inherit computational irreducibility: the only way to know what a rule does is run it. But irreducible processes contain pockets of reducibility — and that is exactly what this graph maps.

The evidence graph is a map of the pockets of reducibility in TE regulation. The refusals mark where the map ends.
